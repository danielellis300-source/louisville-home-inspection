Multi-Family Property Inspections: What Louisville Investors Need to Know

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Louisville has a deep stock of two- to four-unit buildings — the doubles and converted Victorians of Old Louisville, the Highlands, Germantown, and Portland. A multi-family inspection isn't just a single-family inspection times three; the shared systems and conversion history are where the real risk lives.

Shared systems get the most scrutiny

The roof, foundation, main electrical service, common-area plumbing, and any shared heating plant serve every unit, so a failure hits your whole income stream at once. Establish the age and condition of each, how utilities are separated (individually metered vs. owner-paid), and whether a single furnace or boiler heats multiple units — common in older Louisville doubles and a major capital consideration.

Unit sampling and access

A thorough inspector will want into every unit. If a tenant denies access, that unit gets a limited or deferred inspection — note which units were fully seen. In each accessible unit, check the panel or subpanel, HVAC or heat source, water heater, kitchen and bath plumbing, windows, smoke and CO alarms, and means of egress. Patterns across units (the same failed water heater brand in all three) tell you about the last owner's maintenance approach.

Conversion and code history

Many Louisville multis are single-family homes carved into units decades ago, sometimes without permits. Look for undersized egress from basement or attic units, shared or improperly separated HVAC and electrical, missing fire separation between units, and bedrooms without a legal second means of escape. Check with Louisville Metro Codes & Regulations on the legal unit count and permit history — a "triplex" that's legally a single-family is a serious problem.

Sewer, water, and the basement

Multiple kitchens and baths feed one aging lateral, so a sewer scope is essential on any older Louisville multi. In the basement, look at the condition of the common laundry, the water heater array, the main shutoff and branch valves, moisture and the sump system, and whether the framing shows sagging from decades of added load.

Budget the inspection accordingly

A two-to-four-unit inspection takes longer and costs more than a single-family — plan on several hours on site and a fee scaled to unit count, plus the sewer scope. It's still trivial against the cost of discovering an illegal unit or a shared-boiler replacement after closing.

Key takeaways

  • Shared systems — roof, foundation, main service, common plumbing, shared heat — are the highest-stakes items.
  • Get into every unit if possible; note which units were fully inspected and look for cross-unit patterns.
  • Verify legal unit count, permit history, egress, and fire separation with Louisville Metro Codes & Regulations.
  • Always sewer-scope an older multi, and budget for a longer, unit-count-scaled inspection.
