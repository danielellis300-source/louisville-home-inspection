The 10 Most Common Issues We Find in Louisville Home Inspections

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Across the Louisville metro, the same findings come up again and again. None of these should scare you off a house on their own — they're the normal wear and history of local housing stock — but knowing the pattern helps you shop smarter and budget realistically.

1–2. Federal Pacific panels and DIY electrical

Federal Pacific Stab-Lok panels are everywhere in 1950s–1980s Louisville homes and are a recognized fire-safety concern warranting replacement. Right behind them: amateur wiring — open junction boxes, missing covers, over-fused circuits, backstabbed outlets, and ungrounded three-prong receptacles, especially in homes that have passed through several owners.

3–4. Grading and gutter drainage

Soil sloping toward the foundation and downspouts discharging right at the wall are the most common findings we write, period. They're also among the cheapest to fix and the most consequential if ignored, since they feed almost every basement and crawl space moisture problem in the region.

5–6. Aging sewer laterals and older plumbing

Clay-tile and cast iron sewer laterals with root intrusion or corrosion in pre-1970 neighborhoods — and the reminder that the lateral is the homeowner's responsibility in Louisville. Inside the house: galvanized supply lines restricting flow in older homes, and polybutylene in homes built roughly 1978–1995, particularly in south-county and Bullitt County subdivisions.

7–8. Roof age and attic ventilation

Asphalt roofs at or past their service life, often with a second layer over the first, and inadequate attic ventilation and insulation — blocked soffits, bathroom fans dumping into the attic, and insulation well below current levels, which drives ice damming in winter and heat load in summer.

9–10. Crawl space moisture and HVAC at end of life

Crawl space moisture — failed or missing vapor barriers, standing water, rot, and pest activity, driven by clay soil and Ohio Valley humidity. And HVAC systems past their reliable lifespan: original furnaces and air conditioners 15–25 years old, aging heat pumps, and in older homes, boilers and radiator systems that need evaluation rather than assumption.

Honorable mentions

Just outside the top 10: missing GFCI protection in kitchens, baths, and garages; deck ledger boards attached without proper flashing or fasteners; water heaters past 12–15 years or missing a temperature-and-pressure discharge line; wood-to-soil contact inviting termites; and failed window seals. For how these break down by the decade a home was built, see our Jefferson County housing stock guide.

Key takeaways

  • Federal Pacific panels and amateur wiring are the most common electrical findings in the Louisville metro.
  • Grading and gutter drainage top the overall list — cheap to fix, and the source of most moisture problems.
  • Aging clay/cast iron sewer laterals, galvanized supply lines, and 1978–1995 polybutylene are the plumbing pattern.
  • Expect end-of-life roofs and HVAC, poor attic ventilation, and crawl space moisture on older local homes.
