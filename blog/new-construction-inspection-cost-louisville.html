New Construction Inspection Cost in Louisville: Why New Homes Still Need One

July 23, 2026 · 3 min read

New doesn't mean flawless. With subdivisions expanding across Mount Washington, La Grange, Norton Commons, and eastern Oldham and Bullitt Counties every year, we inspect newly built homes constantly — and defects are far more common than most buyers expect from a house nobody has ever lived in.

It's an easy trap to assume a brand-new home from a reputable builder doesn't need the same scrutiny as a resale, especially when the builder's team is already doing walkthroughs. In practice, new construction defects are often just as costly as resale issues — they're simply less expected, which makes catching them early even more valuable.

What it costs

A single final-walkthrough inspection on new construction in Louisville typically runs $325–$475, roughly in line with a resale home of the same size. If you inspect at multiple phases — pre-drywall and final walkthrough, for example — expect to pay for each visit separately, usually $250–$375 per phase visit depending on how far along the home is.

The phase that matters most: pre-drywall

The single highest-value inspection on new construction happens before drywall goes up, when framing, electrical rough-in, plumbing rough-in, and HVAC ductwork are all still fully visible. Once walls are closed, correcting a framing or wiring issue means cutting into finished drywall — so catching it at this stage saves the builder, and by extension you, real money and hassle.

Final walkthrough inspection

Before closing, a full inspection covers the same scope as a resale home: roof, attic, foundation, HVAC operation, electrical panel, plumbing fixtures, grading, and interior finish quality. On new Louisville-area builds we routinely find punch-list items — grading that slopes toward the house, missing insulation baffles, unsealed penetrations, and HVAC systems that were never properly balanced room to room — that the builder's own quality walkthrough missed.

Why the builder's inspector isn't a substitute

Builders typically run their own quality-control walkthrough, but that inspector works for the builder, not you. An independent, buyer-hired inspector has no incentive to soften findings or rush the punch list to hit a closing date. Most builder warranties also require defects to be reported within a specific window (often 30 days at close, then again near the one-year mark) — an independent report gives you clear documentation if a dispute comes up later.

The one-year warranty inspection

Many buyers skip this, and it's a mistake. Scheduling a follow-up inspection close to the one-year builder warranty deadline catches settling cracks, HVAC issues that only show up after a full heating and cooling season, and drainage problems that only appear after real rain — all while they're still the builder's responsibility to fix at no cost to you. This matters especially on lots built over former farmland fill in Bullitt County, where settlement is common.

Key takeaways

  • A final walkthrough inspection on new construction runs $325–$475, similar to a resale home of the same size.
  • The pre-drywall phase is the single highest-value inspection, since framing and rough-in work is still visible.
  • A builder's own quality-control walkthrough is not a substitute for an independent, buyer-hired inspection.
  • A follow-up inspection near the one-year warranty deadline catches issues that only surface after a full season of use.
