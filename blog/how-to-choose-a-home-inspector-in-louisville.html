How to Choose a Home Inspector in Louisville: 7 Red Flags to Avoid

July 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Kentucky licenses home inspectors, which sets a baseline — but the quality gap between a great inspector and a mediocre one is still wide, and you're the one holding the report when it matters. Here are the seven warning signs we tell every Louisville buyer to watch for.

1. An expired, inactive, or unverifiable license

Every home inspector working for compensation in Kentucky must hold a current license under KRS 198B. Ask for the license number and confirm it's active through the state's public lookup. An inspector who's evasive about their license status, or whose license has lapsed, is a hard pass — see our guide to Kentucky home inspector licensing for what the license actually requires.

2. No proof of insurance beyond the state minimum

Kentucky requires at least $250,000 in general liability coverage. Many strong inspectors also carry errors-and-omissions (E&O) coverage, which is what actually responds if a significant defect is missed. Ask to see proof of both. An inspector unwilling to show it is telling you something.

3–4. Vague pricing and no sample report available

A legitimate inspector can quote a firm price based on square footage and requested add-ons before ever showing up. If the price is suspiciously low compared to the going Louisville rate of $300–$500 for a standard home, ask directly what's being cut to hit that number — it's usually time on site or detail in the report.

Every inspector worth hiring can show you a sample report before you book, with no hesitation. If they can't or won't, that's a strong signal the reports themselves are thin — a few checked boxes with no photos or plain-language explanation of what was found and why it matters.

5. An agent who insists on only one inspector

It's normal for a real estate agent to recommend an inspector they trust. It's a problem if the agent insists on only that one and actively discourages you from getting a second opinion — that pattern can signal a referral relationship that prioritizes a smooth, fast-closing transaction over a genuinely thorough report.

6. Discourages you from attending

A confident inspector wants you there. Being present lets you ask questions in real time and see issues firsthand rather than just reading about them later. An inspector who discourages buyers from attending, or schedules at a time they know you can't make, is worth a second look before you commit.

7. Slow, thin reports and poor follow-up

In Louisville, same-day digital reports with photos are the standard, not a premium add-on. A report that takes a week and comes back as a bare checklist is much harder to use in negotiation and harder to reference months later. That same pattern often shows up again after the invoice is paid — a good inspector welcomes follow-up questions for weeks, while a weaker one gets harder to reach. Check reviews specifically for post-inspection responsiveness. For the questions worth asking before you book, see our pre-hire question checklist.

Key takeaways

  • Confirm the inspector's Kentucky license is active and get the number; ask for proof of liability and E&O insurance.
  • A suspiciously low price compared to the going $300–$500 rate usually means less time on site or a thinner report.
  • A legitimate inspector always shows a sample report and welcomes you attending in person.
  • Same-day, photo-rich digital reports are the local standard — and post-inspection responsiveness matters.
