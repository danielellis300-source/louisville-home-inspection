Radon in Kentucky: Do You Really Need a Test Before Buying?

July 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Kentucky doesn't legally require a radon test as part of a home sale. That leaves the decision entirely up to the buyer — and given the geology of the Louisville metro, it's a decision worth making deliberately rather than skipping to save $150 on your inspection invoice.

Because testing is optional rather than mandatory, the decision often gets made by default — buyers simply don't think to ask, and sellers have no obligation to bring it up first. That default matters in a Zone 1 region, since silence on the topic doesn't mean the risk isn't there.

Kentucky has no mandatory radon testing law

There is no Kentucky statute requiring radon testing as a condition of sale. Sellers must disclose a known radon problem or a prior test result on the Seller's Disclosure of Property Conditions form, but there's no legal obligation to test before listing. In practice, radon testing is entirely opt-in for buyers, and a meaningful share of Louisville-area homes are sold without ever being tested.

Why that matters here specifically

The EPA's radon zone map places Jefferson, Oldham, Bullitt, Shelby, Spencer, Nelson, Henry, and Hardin Counties — essentially the entire Louisville metro — in Zone 1, the highest radon potential category. Across the river, Clark and Floyd Counties in Indiana are Zone 1 as well. That doesn't mean every home tests high, but the baseline risk locally is well above the national average, which is exactly the scenario where skipping the test is the wrong call.

Radon doesn't correlate with how the house looks

This surprises most buyers: radon levels have essentially nothing to do with a home's age, condition, or price point. A renovated home in Prospect, a dated ranch in Lyndon, and a mid-century home on a block foundation in Okolona can all test high alike, because radon enters through soil gas and foundation cracks rather than anything visible during a walkthrough. The only way to know is to run the 48-hour test.

Kentucky does regulate radon professionals

While testing isn't mandatory, Kentucky does require radon measurement and mitigation professionals to hold state certification. When you add a radon test to your inspection, ask whether the person placing the monitor is Kentucky-certified, and if a mitigation system is recommended, use a certified mitigation contractor so the installation meets state and EPA standards.

Our recommendation

Add radon testing to every Louisville-area home purchase, full stop. At $125–$175 bundled with your inspection, it's inexpensive insurance against a health risk that's completely fixable — mitigation systems run about $800–$1,500 and are frequently negotiated as a seller-paid item when a high result comes back during due diligence. For pricing details and how the test works, see our radon testing cost guide.

Key takeaways

  • Kentucky has no law requiring radon testing as part of a home sale; sellers must disclose only a known problem or prior result.
  • Jefferson, Oldham, Bullitt, and the surrounding metro counties all fall in EPA Radon Zone 1, the highest-risk category.
  • Radon levels don't correlate with a home's age, price, or visible condition — testing is the only way to know.
  • Kentucky certifies radon measurement and mitigation professionals; use a certified provider for both.
