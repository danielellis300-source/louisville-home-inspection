First-Time Home Buyer's Guide to Inspections in Louisville

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

If you're buying your first home in Louisville, the inspection can feel like a black box: you're told to get one, but not much about how it works or what to do with it. Here's the plain-language version.

When the inspection happens

The inspection comes right after your offer is accepted, during the inspection contingency period defined in your contract — usually the first 7 to 14 days. Schedule it immediately, because that clock is short and popular inspectors book up. You pay for the inspection out of pocket, typically at the time of service, separate from your closing costs.

What it costs and what to add

Budget $300–$500 for a standard Louisville home inspection, plus add-ons. For most purchases we'd recommend at least a radon test ($125–$175 bundled — the whole metro is EPA Zone 1) and, on any home built before roughly 1980 or in an older neighborhood, a sewer scope ($150–$250). A termite/WDO report ($75–$150) is often required by FHA and VA lenders anyway. See our Louisville inspection cost guide for the full breakdown.

Go to the inspection

Attend if you possibly can — ideally the last hour, when the inspector walks you through what they found. You'll learn more about your future home in that hour than in a week of reading. Bring a notepad, wear clothes you don't mind getting dusty, and ask questions freely. There's no dumb question about a house you're about to spend years paying for.

How to read the report without panicking

Your report will be long and it will list problems — that's the job. Sort findings into three buckets: safety and big-ticket systems, routine repairs and maintenance, and cosmetic. A 40-item report with two real concerns is a normal, sound house. Ask the inspector which items they'd prioritize if they were buying it. For what's normal in local housing by era, read our Jefferson County housing stock guide.

Then decide what to do

Within your contingency window you can request repairs, ask for a closing credit or price reduction, bring in a specialist to scope a major item, or walk away. First-time buyers sometimes feel they can't ask for anything — you can, and for safety and major-system items it's expected. Your agent will help you frame the request.

Key takeaways

  • Schedule the inspection the moment your offer is accepted — the contingency window is short.
  • Budget $300–$500 plus a radon test everywhere and a sewer scope on older homes.
  • Attend the inspection, especially the final walkthrough with the inspector.
  • Sort the report into safety, routine, and cosmetic — then negotiate on the first tier within your contingency.
