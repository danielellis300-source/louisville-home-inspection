Fall Home Maintenance Checklist: Getting Your Louisville Home Ready for Winter

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Louisville winters are milder than the upper Midwest, but the Ohio Valley delivers hard freezes, the occasional major ice storm, and enough freeze-thaw cycling to find every weak point in a home's exterior. Fall is when you get ahead of it. Here's the checklist.

Service the heating system

Have the furnace, heat pump, or boiler professionally serviced before the first cold snap. For gas systems, that includes checking the heat exchanger for cracks — a genuine safety item that produces carbon monoxide when it fails. Replace filters, test the thermostat, and confirm carbon monoxide detectors work and are less than seven years old. Older Louisville homes with cast iron radiators should have the boiler pressure and the radiator vents checked.

Gutters, roof, and the tree canopy

Clean gutters again after the leaves finish dropping — Louisville's canopy means a second cleaning in late fall is not optional. Check the roof and flashing while the weather is still mild, and trim tree limbs that overhang the roof or power service drop. Ice storms bring limbs down, and a proactive trim is far cheaper than emergency tree service and roof repair in January.

Freeze protection for plumbing

Disconnect and drain garden hoses, shut off and drain exterior hose bibs (or confirm frost-free sillcocks are functioning), and insulate exposed pipes in the crawl space, garage, and along exterior walls. In older homes, pipes running through an uninsulated crawl space or an unheated addition are the ones that burst during a hard freeze. Know where your main water shutoff is before you need it at 2 a.m.

Seal the envelope

Add weatherstripping and door sweeps where you can feel a draft, and caulk gaps around window and door trim, penetrations, and where different materials meet. Check attic insulation depth — many older Louisville homes are well under current recommendations — and make sure soffit vents aren't blocked, so the attic stays cold and dry and you don't get ice damming at the eaves during a snow event.

Chimney and fireplace

If you burn wood, have the chimney swept and inspected for creosote buildup and liner cracks. If you have a gas fireplace, confirm it lights cleanly and the venting is clear. On older homes with a masonry chimney, check for spalling brick and a cracked or missing crown — freeze-thaw does real damage to chimneys over a Louisville winter.

Key takeaways

  • Service the heating system before the first freeze, including a heat-exchanger check on gas furnaces.
  • Clean gutters a second time after full leaf drop, and trim limbs overhanging the roof and service drop before ice-storm season.
  • Drain exterior hose bibs and insulate pipes in crawl spaces, garages, and unheated additions.
  • Top up attic insulation and keep soffit vents clear to prevent ice dams during snow events.
