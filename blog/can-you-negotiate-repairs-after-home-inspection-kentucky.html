Can You Negotiate Repairs After a Home Inspection in Kentucky?

July 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Yes — in most Kentucky residential transactions, the inspection is exactly the point at which repairs, credits, and price get renegotiated. How much leverage you have depends on your contract, the market, and how you frame the request. Here's how it works in practice around Louisville.

It starts with the inspection contingency

Most Kentucky purchase contracts include an inspection contingency giving the buyer a defined window — often 7 to 14 days — to inspect the property and respond. Within that window you can typically request repairs or concessions, ask for more time to investigate a specific issue, or terminate the contract and recover your earnest money, depending on the exact contingency language. Read that section with your agent before you order the inspection so you know your deadlines.

What you can reasonably ask for

The strongest requests focus on safety issues and major-system defects: active roof leaks, a failed or unsafe electrical panel, non-functioning HVAC, plumbing leaks, structural movement, a compromised sewer lateral, or significant water intrusion. Asking a seller to address a Federal Pacific panel, a bad sewer line, or a roof at end of life is normal and expected in the Louisville market. Asking them to re-caulk every window or fix cosmetic drywall cracks generally isn't, and a long nitpicky list can make a seller dig in.

Repairs vs. credit vs. price reduction

Sellers usually respond with one of three things: agree to complete specific repairs before closing, offer a closing cost credit so you handle the work yourself, or reduce the sale price. Many Louisville buyers prefer a credit or price reduction — you control the contractor and the quality, and seller repairs done under deadline are sometimes just enough to check the box. Note that lenders limit how large a seller credit can be relative to the price, so big concessions may be split between a credit and a price cut.

How market conditions change your leverage

In a slower market with more inventory, sellers are more willing to negotiate repairs and credits. In a hot micro-market — a well-priced house in Crescent Hill or the Cherokee Triangle with multiple offers — you may have less room, and some buyers waive minor requests to keep the deal clean. Your agent's read on the specific street and price point matters here.

Get quotes before you negotiate a number

If you're asking for money instead of repairs, back it with a real contractor estimate rather than a guess. "The report says the roof is aging" is weak; "here's a $14,200 quote to replace the roof" is a number the seller's agent can work with. For a bigger-picture view of your options when a report comes back rough, see what happens after a bad inspection report.

Key takeaways

  • The inspection contingency in your Kentucky contract defines your window and your rights — read it before ordering the inspection.
  • Focus requests on safety and major-system defects; long cosmetic lists weaken your position.
  • Credits and price reductions let you control repair quality; seller repairs get the work done for you.
  • Back any dollar request with a real contractor quote, and factor in how competitive the specific listing is.
