Summer Home Maintenance in Kentucky: Fighting Humidity, Mold, and HVAC Overload

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Louisville summers are hot and, more importantly, humid. Ohio Valley dew points sit in the 70s for weeks, and that moisture is what drives the mold, musty odors, and swollen-door complaints we hear about every August. Managing summer humidity is mostly about keeping moist air out of the parts of the house that stay cool.

Control crawl space and basement moisture

Warm, humid outdoor air entering a cool crawl space or basement condenses on framing, ductwork, and cold water lines — the classic recipe for mold. Keep a hygrometer in the basement and aim to hold relative humidity below 55–60%. A properly sized dehumidifier with a condensate pump is one of the best investments a Louisville homeowner can make. If your crawl space is vented and chronically damp, ask an inspector or contractor whether encapsulation makes sense for your home.

Don't oversize or overwork the AC

An oversized air conditioner cools the house quickly but short-cycles before it removes much humidity, leaving the interior cool and clammy. If your home feels damp even when the thermostat is satisfied, that's often the cause. Keep the filter clean, keep the outdoor coil clear, and have the system checked if it can't hold both temperature and humidity during a heat wave.

Check the condensate drain

Your AC pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid Louisville day, and it all leaves through the condensate drain line. A clogged line backs up into the furnace or air handler, the drain pan overflows, and you get water damage and mold in a closet or ceiling. Flush the line at the start of summer and again mid-season, and make sure the secondary drain pan and float switch are in place.

Ventilate the wet rooms

Run bathroom exhaust fans during and for 15–20 minutes after every shower, and confirm they vent outside rather than into the attic. Use the kitchen range hood when cooking. In laundry rooms, make sure the dryer vents fully to the exterior with a short, clean duct run — a restricted dryer vent adds humidity and is a fire hazard.

Watch the exterior, too

Humidity plus Louisville's tree cover keeps the north side of many homes damp. Keep shrubs trimmed back from siding for airflow, clear gutters so they don't overflow against the wall, and address any sprinkler heads spraying the foundation or siding. Persistent exterior dampness eventually finds its way to interior moisture and rot.

Key takeaways

  • Hold basement and crawl space humidity below 55–60% with a properly sized dehumidifier and condensate pump.
  • An oversized AC short-cycles and leaves the house cool but clammy — humidity control is part of correct sizing.
  • Flush the AC condensate drain at the start and middle of summer to prevent overflow and mold.
  • Vent bathrooms, kitchen, and dryer fully to the exterior — never into the attic.
