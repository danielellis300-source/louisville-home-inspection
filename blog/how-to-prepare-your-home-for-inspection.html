How to Prepare Your Home for a Buyer's Inspection: A Seller's Checklist

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

You can't control what an inspector finds, but you can control how smoothly the inspection goes — and a smooth, well-prepared inspection tends to produce a shorter, less alarming list. Here's how Louisville sellers should get ready.

Clear access to everything

The fastest way to a bad report is a system the inspector couldn't fully evaluate. Clear a path to the electrical panel, water heater, furnace, and the crawl space or attic access. Move boxes off the basement walls, unblock the HVAC returns, and make sure the inspector can open the garage, get to the sump pit, and reach every exterior wall. Anything inaccessible gets noted as "unable to inspect," which makes buyers nervous.

Handle the cheap stuff before they arrive

Replace burned-out bulbs (so "light inoperable" doesn't imply a wiring problem), swap dead smoke and CO detector batteries, replace the HVAC filter, and re-caulk tubs and windows where it's failed. Fix running toilets, dripping faucets, and loose toilet seats. None of these are major, but a report full of small items reads as deferred maintenance and invites the buyer to look harder.

Address safety items directly

Inspectors and buyers react strongest to safety findings: missing GFCI protection near water, a double-tapped breaker, missing stair or deck railings, reversed hot/neutral wiring at outlets, and gaps around the water heater or furnace flue. If you know about any of these, fixing them beforehand removes the scariest lines from the report. In Louisville, a Federal Pacific panel is worth addressing or at least pricing out before listing.

Deal with water and drainage

Extend downspouts away from the foundation, clean the gutters, and regrade obvious low spots against the house. Run a dehumidifier in the basement or crawl space in the days before the inspection so it doesn't smell musty. If you've had past water intrusion and fixed it, have the paperwork ready — a documented, repaired issue is far less alarming than a surprise damp corner.

Be somewhere else, and leave documentation

Plan to be out of the house for the full inspection window, pets included. Leave out any records that help: recent roof or HVAC invoices, the radon mitigation certificate, termite treatment history, and permits for past work. And complete your Kentucky disclosure form carefully and honestly — see our guide to the disclosure law — since inconsistencies between the form and the report create their own problems.

Key takeaways

  • Clear access to the panel, water heater, HVAC, attic, and crawl space so nothing gets marked "unable to inspect."
  • Fix cheap items — bulbs, detector batteries, running toilets, failed caulk — so the report doesn't read as neglect.
  • Resolve or price out safety findings in advance, including a Federal Pacific panel if you have one.
  • Manage drainage and basement humidity, and leave maintenance records and permits out for the buyer.
