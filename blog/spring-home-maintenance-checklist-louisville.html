Spring Home Maintenance Checklist for Louisville Homeowners

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Spring in Louisville means heavy rain, the occasional severe thunderstorm or tornado watch, and the first real test of how your home handles water. A few hours of maintenance now heads off the problems that turn into inspection findings — and expensive repairs — later. Here's the checklist we'd run on our own homes.

Roof, gutters, and drainage

After winter, check the roof from the ground with binoculars for lifted, cracked, or missing shingles and damaged flashing. Clean gutters and downspouts — Louisville's mature tree canopy fills them fast — and make sure downspouts discharge at least four to six feet from the foundation. Standing water against the foundation after a spring storm is the single most common cause of the basement and crawl space moisture we write up all year.

Grading and the lot

Walk the perimeter and look for soil that has settled against the foundation or slopes toward the house. Re-slope low spots so water runs away from the structure. In the east end and Oldham and Bullitt Counties, watch for new depressions, soft spots, or circular cracks in the yard — early signs of karst or sinkhole activity worth monitoring and, if they grow, having evaluated.

Test the sump pump before you need it

Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and confirm the pump switches on, moves water, and shuts off. Check that the discharge line is clear and not frozen or crushed. If your home is in a low-lying area near Beargrass Creek, Pond Creek, or the river, consider a battery backup or water-powered backup — spring storms and power outages tend to arrive together.

Get ahead of cooling season

Replace the HVAC filter, clear leaves and debris from around the outdoor condenser, and gently rinse the coil fins. Schedule an AC tune-up before the first 90-degree stretch, when every HVAC company in town is booked solid. Pour water down rarely-used floor drains and check that the condensate drain line from the furnace or air handler is flowing and not clogged.

Exterior wood, paint, and seals

Inspect decks, porches, and railings for rot, loose fasteners, and wobble — Louisville's humidity is hard on exterior wood. Check exterior caulk and paint, especially on older homes with wood siding and trim, and reseal gaps around windows and doors. Look for wood-to-soil contact anywhere on the structure; in a heavy termite-pressure area, that's an open invitation.

Key takeaways

  • Clean gutters and extend downspouts before spring storms — foundation water is the root of most moisture findings.
  • Re-slope soil that has settled toward the house, and watch east-end yards for early karst or sinkhole signs.
  • Test the sump pump with a bucket of water now, and consider a backup if you're in a low-lying area.
  • Service the AC and clear the condensate line before the first heat wave, when contractors are overbooked.
