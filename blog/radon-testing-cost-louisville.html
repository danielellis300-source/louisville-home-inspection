Radon Testing Cost in Louisville: What to Expect and Why It's Worth It

July 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Radon is invisible, odorless, and the second-leading cause of lung cancer in the United States after smoking. Most of the Louisville metro sits in an EPA zone where elevated levels are common. Here's what a radon test actually costs in Louisville, how the process works, and how to make sense of the number that comes back.

Radon almost always comes up during a real estate transaction rather than as a routine homeowner check-in, which means the test has to fit inside an already tight inspection contingency window. Building it into your first inspection appointment, rather than as an afterthought once the rest of the report comes back, keeps your timeline intact.

What a radon test costs

Bundled with a home inspection, a radon test in Louisville typically runs $125–$175. As a standalone service scheduled separately, expect closer to $150–$225, since the technician has to make a dedicated trip to place and later retrieve the monitor. Bundling with your full inspection is almost always the better value, and results come back on the same general timeline as the rest of your report.

How the test actually works

A continuous radon monitor is placed in the lowest livable level of the home — usually the basement — and left running for a minimum 48-hour period. The home needs "closed house conditions" during that window: windows shut, exterior doors used normally, HVAC running as it normally would. Opening windows or running whole-house fans during the test can invalidate the results, so if you're the seller and a test is underway, mention it to whoever's still living there.

Reading your results

The EPA action level is 4.0 pCi/L. Results below that are considered acceptable, though the EPA notes there's no truly "safe" level and mitigation can be worth considering between 2 and 4 pCi/L, especially for a household with children or anyone with respiratory concerns. Results above 4.0 pCi/L warrant a radon mitigation system, which typically costs $800–$1,500 installed in the Louisville area and can often be negotiated into the purchase agreement as a seller-paid item.

Why Louisville-area homes test high so often

Jefferson, Oldham, Bullitt, Shelby, Spencer, and Nelson Counties all fall within EPA Radon Zone 1, the highest potential category, largely due to the region's limestone and karst geology. It's common for homes on opposite sides of the same street to test differently, since radon migrates through soil gas and enters through foundation cracks, sump pits, and slab penetrations. A neighbor's clean test tells you little about your own home, and a test from several years ago shouldn't be assumed still accurate.

Kentucky also certifies radon measurement and mitigation professionals under state law, so ask whether your tester holds current Kentucky radon certification.

Should every buyer test?

Yes. Radon mitigation is one of the few inspection findings that's genuinely cheap to fix relative to the health risk of ignoring it, and testing adds only a modest amount to your total inspection invoice. On a cost-benefit basis, it's one of the easiest add-ons to justify of any service we offer.

Key takeaways

  • A bundled radon test in Louisville costs $125–$175; a standalone test runs $150–$225.
  • The test requires a continuous 48-hour monitoring period under closed-house conditions.
  • The EPA action level is 4.0 pCi/L, and Jefferson, Oldham, and Bullitt Counties all sit in Zone 1.
  • Mitigation systems typically run $800–$1,500 and can often be negotiated as a seller-paid repair.
