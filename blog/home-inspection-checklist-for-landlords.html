Home Inspection Checklist for Louisville Landlords and Property Managers

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

For rental owners, inspections aren't just a purchase-time event — they're an ongoing risk-management tool. A consistent inspection routine protects the asset, limits liability, and keeps you ahead of code enforcement. Here's how Louisville landlords should structure it.

Acquisition inspection: before you buy

Treat a rental purchase inspection at least as seriously as a personal-home purchase, and add: full sewer scope (deferred maintenance is common on rentals), a hard look at the electrical panel and any prior DIY wiring, water heater and HVAC age across every unit, and roof life expectancy. Price the near-term capital needs — roof, HVAC, sewer, panel — into your offer, because these are the items that wreck a rental's cash flow in year one.

Know the local rental rules

Jefferson County / Louisville Metro has adopted the International Property Maintenance Code, and Metro Codes & Regulations enforces habitability standards through the Property Maintenance Division. Some Kentucky jurisdictions and the Metro government have specific rental registration or inspection programs; confirm current requirements for your property's location before you close, and again if rules change. An inspection organized around IPMC habitability items (heat, hot water, weathertight envelope, working smoke/CO alarms, safe egress, no active leaks) keeps you defensible.

Turnover inspection: between tenants

At every turnover, document condition with dated photos: walls, floors, appliances, windows, smoke and CO alarms (test and date them), water heater, HVAC filter, plumbing under every sink, and the exterior. This is your security-deposit evidence and your maintenance-planning tool. Catching a slow under-sink leak at turnover instead of six months in is the difference between a $150 fix and a $4,000 subfloor and mold job.

Periodic inspection: during tenancy

Kentucky's landlord-tenant framework generally requires reasonable notice before entry (commonly 48 hours) except in emergencies, and many Louisville leases specify it. Schedule periodic interior inspections — quarterly to semi-annually is typical — to check for unreported leaks, HVAC filter neglect, unauthorized occupants or pets, and safety items. Put the right and the cadence in the lease so it's expected, not a surprise.

Seasonal exterior checks

Twice a year, walk the exterior of every property: gutters, downspout extensions, grading, roof from the ground, exterior stairs and railings, and the crawl space or basement for moisture. Louisville's humidity and tree canopy make deferred exterior maintenance escalate fast.

Key takeaways

  • At acquisition, add a sewer scope and price roof, HVAC, sewer, and panel replacement into your offer.
  • Organize inspections around IPMC habitability items; confirm Louisville Metro rental requirements for your property.
  • Document every turnover with dated photos — it's both deposit evidence and a maintenance plan.
  • Give proper notice (commonly 48 hours) and run periodic interior checks plus twice-yearly exterior walks.
