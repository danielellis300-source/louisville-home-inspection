How Much Does a Home Inspection Cost in Louisville? (2026 Price Guide)

July 23, 2026 · 4 min read

If you're buying a home in Louisville, the inspection is one of the smallest numbers on your closing statement and one of the highest-leverage. A $375 inspection can surface $18,000 in deferred roof, sewer, or HVAC work before you're legally committed to it. Here's what inspections actually cost across the Louisville metro right now, what's included, and what moves the price up or down.

Typical Louisville home inspection pricing

A standard full home inspection in Louisville and Jefferson County generally runs $300 to $500, with most single-family homes between 1,400 and 2,600 square feet landing around $350–$425. Condos and smaller shotgun houses in Germantown or Old Louisville often come in under $325, while larger homes in Prospect, Anchorage, and eastern Oldham County can run $450–$700+ once square footage, outbuildings, and add-on tests are factored in.

Pricing is almost always based on square footage and age rather than a flat rate. A 1,100 sq ft bungalow in the Highlands and an 1,100 sq ft ranch in Okolona should cost about the same, all else equal. If a quote seems disconnected from the home's actual size, ask what it's based on before booking.

What's included in a standard inspection fee

The base fee covers a full visual inspection of the roof, attic and insulation, structural components, foundation, basement or crawl space, exterior grading and drainage, HVAC, plumbing, electrical panel and wiring, and every interior room, plus a same-day digital report with photos. A typical visit runs 2–4 hours on site, with the written report usually delivered the same evening.

It does not automatically include radon testing, sewer scope, a termite/WDO report, or well and septic evaluation. Those are priced separately because they require extra equipment, lab time, or a longer on-site window. Always ask for the full scope, not just the base number, before comparing quotes between inspectors.

Common add-on tests and their cost

  • Radon testing: typically $125–$175 bundled, run over a continuous 48-hour period. Jefferson, Oldham, and Bullitt Counties are all in EPA Radon Zone 1, so this one is worth it on nearly every purchase.
  • Sewer scope inspection: typically $150–$250 depending on line length and cleanout access — high priority in Old Louisville, the Highlands, Clifton, and Germantown.
  • Termite / wood-destroying organism (WDO) report: typically $75–$150, and frequently required by lenders on FHA and VA loans in Kentucky.
  • Well and septic inspection: $150–$400, common on the rural edges of Oldham and Bullitt Counties and in parts of Anchorage.

Bundling add-ons with your main inspection is almost always cheaper than a separate visit later, since the inspector is already on site. Ask for an all-in quote up front rather than pricing each item after the fact.

What makes Louisville pricing go up

Five things reliably push the quote higher: square footage over 3,000 sq ft; a crawl space or unfinished basement that has to be physically entered rather than viewed from a hatch; homes built before 1940 (Old Louisville, the older Highlands, downtown New Albany) that may have knob-and-tube wiring, plaster, and original systems needing extra attention; detached structures like a carriage house or pole barn; and karst or floodplain lots where foundation and drainage conditions take longer to document.

Homes on private well and septic on the outer edges of the metro also run higher, since each of those systems is essentially its own mini-inspection. Spring and early summer, the busiest local buying season, can also mean less flexibility on same-week scheduling — booking a day or two earlier than you think you need to rarely hurts.

Is the cheapest inspector actually the best deal?

Rarely. A rock-bottom price often signals a rushed inspection, an inspector cutting time on site, or a report with no photos. In a $300,000-plus purchase, the difference between a $300 inspector and a $450 inspector is a rounding error — but the difference between a thorough report and a thin one can cost you tens of thousands after closing.

Kentucky does license home inspectors, which sets a floor, but license status alone doesn't tell you how detailed the report will be. Ask what a quote excludes, not just what it includes, and see our guide on choosing a home inspector in Louisville for the specific red flags to watch for.

Key takeaways

  • A standard Louisville home inspection runs $300–$500, with most 1,400–2,600 sq ft homes landing around $350–$425.
  • Radon, sewer scope, termite/WDO, and well/septic evaluation are separate add-ons, not part of the base fee.
  • Pre-1940 homes, homes over 3,000 sq ft, and karst or floodplain lots take longer to inspect and cost more.
  • The cheapest quote in town is rarely the best value once you factor in report quality and time on site.
