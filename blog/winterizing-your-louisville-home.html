Winterizing Your Louisville Home: A Room-by-Room Checklist

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

Winterizing works best as a walk-through: go room by room with a caulk gun, a flashlight, and a notepad. Louisville's winters aren't brutal, but a single hard freeze finds every gap. Here's the room-by-room version.

Kitchen and bathrooms

Open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze so household heat reaches the pipes. Check for drafts around plumbing penetrations and seal them with expanding foam or caulk. Confirm bathroom exhaust fans actually vent to the exterior, not into the attic — a surprisingly common defect that dumps moist air into cold framing all winter and causes mold.

Basement and crawl space

This is where Louisville homes are most vulnerable. Insulate exposed water supply lines, close or insulate crawl space vents for the season if your crawl space is vented, and confirm the vapor barrier on the crawl space floor is intact. Check that the sump pump works and its discharge line won't freeze. Look for daylight around the rim joist and seal it — a huge amount of winter heat loss happens right there.

Attic

Verify insulation depth and even coverage, and make sure it isn't blocking soffit vents. Check for gaps around the attic hatch, recessed lights, and the chimney chase, and seal them so warm, moist air from the house doesn't rise into the attic and condense. Proper attic sealing and ventilation is what prevents ice dams at the eaves during a snow event.

Living areas and bedrooms

Feel for drafts around windows and exterior doors and add weatherstripping, door sweeps, and rope caulk as needed. Reverse ceiling fans to clockwise on low to push warm air down. On older homes with original single-pane windows, interior storm inserts or a quality film kit make a real comfort difference without touching the historic sash.

Exterior and additions

Walk the outside: drain and cover hose bibs, clear gutters, and check that the grading and downspouts move snowmelt away from the foundation. Pay special attention to unheated or minimally heated additions and sunrooms — their plumbing and the pipes feeding them are the most likely to freeze in a Louisville cold snap. If an addition has its own crawl space, it needs the same pipe insulation and vent treatment as the main one.

Key takeaways

  • Under-sink cabinets on exterior walls should be opened during a hard freeze; seal plumbing penetrations year-round.
  • The crawl space is the highest-risk zone — insulate pipes, protect the sump discharge, and seal the rim joist.
  • Air-seal the attic hatch, recessed lights, and chimney chase to prevent condensation and ice dams.
  • Unheated additions and sunrooms are the most freeze-prone plumbing in a Louisville home.
