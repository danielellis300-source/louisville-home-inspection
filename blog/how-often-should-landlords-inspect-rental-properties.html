How Often Should Landlords Inspect Rental Properties in Kentucky?

July 23, 2026 · 2 min read

There's no Kentucky statute that dictates a specific rental inspection schedule, so the right cadence is a judgment call balancing asset protection against tenants' right to quiet enjoyment. Here's a practical framework for Louisville-area landlords.

A workable baseline schedule

  • Move-in and move-out: every time, with a detailed dated photo record. Non-negotiable.
  • Interior periodic: every 6 months is a reasonable default; quarterly for higher-risk properties (student rentals, past problem tenancies, older systems).
  • Exterior / grounds: spring and fall at minimum — Louisville's freeze-thaw and humidity punish deferred exterior maintenance.
  • Post-event: after any major storm, hard freeze, or reported leak.

The notice rules matter

Kentucky's Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, where adopted (Louisville Metro and Jefferson County have adopted it), generally requires the landlord to give at least two days' notice and enter at reasonable times, except in an emergency. Put your inspection right and cadence in the lease, give written notice every time, and keep a copy. Entering without notice is how a routine inspection turns into a legal problem.

Don't over-inspect

Monthly interior inspections read as harassment, damage the tenant relationship, and can expose you to claims of interfering with quiet enjoyment. If you feel you need to be inside every month, that's usually a screening or lease-enforcement problem, not an inspection-frequency problem.

What to actually check on a periodic visit

Smoke and CO alarms (test and date), HVAC filter, under-sink plumbing for leaks, water heater and area around it, window and door operation, signs of moisture or mold, unauthorized pets or occupants, and any tenant-reported item. Keep it to 15–20 minutes and leave a summary of what you looked at.

Bring in a professional periodically

Every few years, or before a major lease renewal on a long tenancy, a professional inspection of the building systems — roof, structure, electrical, HVAC, plumbing, crawl space — catches the slow deterioration that a quick interior walk misses. It also gives you an objective condition baseline if a dispute ever arises.

Key takeaways

  • Always inspect at move-in and move-out with dated photos; interior periodic checks every 6 months is a solid default.
  • Walk the exterior each spring and fall, and after any major storm, freeze, or reported leak.
  • Give the notice Kentucky's landlord-tenant act requires (at least two days where adopted) and put the cadence in the lease.
  • Avoid monthly interior visits, and commission a professional systems inspection every few years.
