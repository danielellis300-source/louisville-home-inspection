Kentucky Home Inspector Licensing: What the Law Requires and What Else Matters

July 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Unlike some neighboring states, Kentucky does require home inspectors to hold a state license. That gives Louisville buyers a real baseline of protection — but a license is a floor, not a ceiling, and there's still meaningful vetting to do before you hire one.

Understanding what the license actually requires helps you use it well: it tells you what a licensed inspector has already proven, and what it doesn't guarantee about the quality of the report you'll receive.

Kentucky licenses home inspectors under KRS 198B

The Kentucky Board of Home Inspectors, established under KRS 198B.700 through 198B.738, regulates the profession. To be licensed, an inspector must be at least 18, hold a high school diploma or equivalent, complete 80 hours of board-approved pre-licensing education, pass the National Home Inspector Examination (NHIE), and carry a general liability insurance policy of at least $250,000. Licensees must also complete continuing education — currently 20 hours per two-year renewal cycle — to stay active.

How to verify an inspector's license

Kentucky maintains a public license lookup through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Before you book, confirm the inspector's license is active and in good standing, and note the license number on your agreement. Any inspector performing home inspections for compensation in Kentucky without a current license is operating unlawfully — that alone should end the conversation.

What the license doesn't tell you

The license confirms baseline education, a passing exam score, and minimum insurance. It doesn't tell you how many inspections the person has actually performed, how detailed and photo-rich their reports are, whether they physically enter crawl spaces and walk roofs, or how responsive they are to follow-up questions after the invoice is paid. Those are the differences between a genuinely useful report and a thin one, and you have to ask about them directly.

Certifications that go beyond the state minimum

Many strong Kentucky inspectors also carry InterNACHI or ASHI membership. Both require adherence to a published standards of practice and ongoing education beyond what the state mandates. It's not a substitute for the license, but an inspector who has voluntarily opted into a national standard on top of Kentucky's requirements is signaling how seriously they take the work.

What to ask beyond 'are you licensed?'

Since the answer in Kentucky should always be yes, move past it quickly: Is your license current, and what's the number? How many inspections have you completed? Do you also carry errors-and-omissions coverage on top of the required liability policy? Do you enter crawl spaces and walk roofs when safe? Can I see a sample report? Key takeaways

  • Kentucky requires home inspectors to be licensed under KRS 198B.700–738, administered by the Kentucky Board of Home Inspectors.
  • Licensing requires 80 hours of pre-licensing education, passing the NHIE, and at least $250,000 in general liability insurance.
  • Verify the license is active through the state's public lookup and record the number on your agreement.
  • The license is a baseline — report quality, experience, and thoroughness still have to be vetted separately.
